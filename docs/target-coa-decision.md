# Target COA decision — 2 October 2026

## Decision

The five-digit chart in `YIFRE_Posting_Engine_Final_Implementation_Ready_01102026.xlsx` is the engineering target. This follows the user's direction on 2 October 2026 to proceed with the recommendation to use the workbook identified in the repository as the approved posting-engine source.

This is an engineering target selection. It is **not** Finance approval of account classifications, mappings, opening balances, or migration. The existing `approved-coa-crosswalk-review.csv` is explicitly a standards-based recommendation: all 68 rows still have blank Finance-approved target codes and no Finance review status.

## Why code migration is not a simple code-length change

- The workbook defines 134 five-digit accounts and also supplies posting-account, control-account, manual-journal, normal-balance, and cost-centre metadata. Provisioning must honor those attributes rather than create every account as an active posting account.
- The application chart roles currently refer to six-digit target accounts. Some correspond to a single workbook account, but others require a subledger-driven choice or split. Examples include raw material versus feed inventory, PPE and accumulated depreciation by asset class, livestock by species and maturity, harvested eggs versus viable eggs, and payroll/levy liabilities by beneficiary and nature.
- The existing chart-unification process moves four-digit legacy balances to six-digit codes. Moving it to five-digit targets changes its account map, journal descriptions, post-cutover checks, configuration repointing, account lifecycle rules, and test expectations.
- Workbook account-map and posting-key references must resolve to actual five-digit GL account IDs while keeping dynamic service-resolved postings dimensioned correctly.

The role-by-role findings are in [target-coa-role-crosswalk.md](target-coa-role-crosswalk.md). It records safe direct candidates separately from mappings that depend on item class, asset class, livestock stage, cost centre, tax/liability nature, or transaction source.

## The cost-centre rule, measured and enforced (17 October 2026)

The earlier note said 447 control lines carry a cost-centre requirement and none was enforced. Both halves were loose. The precise counts are **306 Required, 222 Inherited, 141 Not required**, and the per-line columns hold no per-rule nuance at all — one value each, repeated:

- **Cost Centre Source**, on all 306: "Source document, item, batch or work centre"
- **Cost Centre Validation**, on all 306: "Active; Posting Allowed = Yes; application matches or approved YifrehCore shared centre"

So the enforceable content is one rule in three clauses, and two of them were already enforced on every posting. `DimensionValidatorService` refuses a cost centre that is inactive, that belongs to another company, or that the approved master marks non-posting. Account-level presence is enforced too: `requiresCostCentre` comes from the workbook's own Cost Centre Rule column (104 of the 137 accounts) and a line that omits one is refused.

The third clause — **application matches** — was not enforced, and that was the real gap. A poultry cost could be charged to a snail cost centre and nothing objected: the journal balances, the trial balance balances, and the error surfaces in the segment report at the year end, by which time it is a restatement rather than a correction.

It is enforced now. The workbook's centres carry their application (SNP-* YifrehSnail, PLP-* YifrehPoultry, COR-* and FDM-* YifrehCore), and a line that carries both a `speciesKey` and a cost centre must use that business's own centre or a shared YifrehCore one. Centres with no application — every centre on the four- and six-digit charts — are left alone: this is the approved chart's rule, and inventing a classification for a centre nobody classified would refuse postings a farm has always made.

The full integration suite passes unchanged (61 files, 620 tests), so no flow the suite covers was charging across businesses.

## Remaining code-to-workbook gaps found in the continued audit (10 November 2026)

- Statutory payroll accrual (`PayrollRun`) and payroll payment (`PayrollPayment`) still call the GL posting service without an approved event contract. The workbook's `COR-YFR-063A` is only eligible labour expense against payroll payable; it does not describe a full statutory accrual or bank settlement. The approved chart has no distinct mapped keys for PAYE, employee/employer pension, NHF, NSITF and ITF liabilities, and currently has only generic `PAYROLL_PAYABLE` and `ACCRUAL_CONTROL` mappings. Binding the full payroll journal to 063A or substituting those generic accounts would be unsupported. Finance needs to approve the missing liability account keys and event directions before those contracts can be completed.
- `REARING_COST_RELIEF` (mortality/disposal/harvest weighted-average rearing cost outflow) still posts without an approved control. It is distinct from the fair-value biological-asset mortality events `COR-YFR-035/036`; those rules debit normal/abnormal biological loss and credit biological-asset control, while this service clears accumulated cohort rearing cost. Its correct expense/COGS treatment and mortality classification need an approved event basis before binding.
- Approved capitalized variance proration reversal now has its own contract (`COR-APP-VAR-PRORATE-REV-001`). It checks the exact reversed account-side amounts against the source journal and verifies all source accounts remain actively mapped.

These are not database migration tasks. They are incomplete contract/accounting decisions in code and source mappings; no production data was changed while identifying them. Manual journals remain governed by their own approval and account-policy path, so their lack of a workbook event binding is not automatically a defect.
